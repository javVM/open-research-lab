## Sources deliberately not used

- Aggregator "pricing/review" sites (Capterra, GetApp, ITQlick) were consulted only to confirm
  that a vendor's pricing is per-seat and in what band. Their figures are inconsistent with each
  other and with vendor pages, so no number from them is quoted as FACT.
- A dictionary-style page asserting that "a 2024 audit of a 600-litre −80 °C freezer found 38% of
  boxes were abandoned" was found but is unattributed and of unverifiable provenance. It is
  excluded; the freezer-abandonment problem is instead supported by S21 and S22, which are
  attributable. This matters: the number is exactly the kind of quotable statistic that would
  damage our credibility if a researcher checked it.

## Known gaps in this research

- **No published quantitative survey of location-tracking failure rates** in either small labs or
  small collections was found. The pain is documented qualitatively and by proxy (S2, S3, S20,
  S22), not measured. This is the single biggest evidentiary weakness in the market analysis and
  the main reason user interviews are non-optional.
- **The iDigBio 2025 small-collections survey results (S4) are not yet published.** They are the
  most directly relevant dataset in existence for market B/C and should be checked before any
  build decision is finalised.
- **No pricing evidence at all** for what a small collection or small lab would pay for a *local*
  tool, because no such product is on the market to price.
- Sources skew heavily towards the US and Europe (Index Herbariorum and GBIF are global; iDigBio,
  SPNHC, PDWG, Specify, Arctos and Symbiota are US-centred). Spanish- and Latin
  American-language communities were not searched and are a plausible under-served segment worth
  a dedicated look, given the maintainer's language.
